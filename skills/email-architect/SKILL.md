# Email Architect

## Overview
Generates polished, effective email copy for outreach, follow-ups, and campaigns. Adapts tone, length, and CTA based on audience and context — from cold outreach to warm lead nurturing.

## When to Use
- Drafting cold outreach emails for sales prospecting
- Writing follow-up sequences after meetings, demos, or proposals
- Creating email templates for marketing campaigns, drip sequences, or newsletters
- Rephrasing existing emails for better clarity, punch, or conversion
- A/B testing email variants with different tones or CTAs

## How It Works
1. Identify the audience, goal, and stage in the funnel (cold, warm, hot).
2. Choose an appropriate tone: professional, casual, urgent, empathetic.
3. Draft the email with a strong subject line, concise body, and clear CTA.
4. Include personalization hooks where possible (name, company, pain point, recent trigger event).
5. Format for readability: short paragraphs, bullet points, bold key phrases (sparingly).

## Steps
1. **Clarify context** — Ask for: recipient role, relationship stage, goal of the email, and any specific talking points.
2. **Choose template or fresh draft** — Either select from known patterns (PAS, AIDA, BAB) or write from scratch.
3. **Draft subject line** — Keep it under 50 chars, spark curiosity or convey value, avoid spam triggers.
4. **Write body** — Hook (first line), value proposition, social proof or context, CTA. Keep it 3-5 short paragraphs.
5. **Polish** — Remove filler words, check for passive voice, ensure one clear CTA.
6. **Generate variants** — Offer 2-3 subject line alternatives and optional body tweaks.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Too long**: Overly verbose emails get skimmed or deleted. Keep it tight.
- **Weak CTA**: Every email needs a single, clear next step. Don't bury it.
- **Generic first line**: "I hope this email finds you well" kills engagement. Open with context or value.
- **No personalization**: Generic blasts perform poorly. Always include at least one personal element.
- **Spam triggers**: Avoid excessive caps, multiple exclamation points, and trigger words like "free", "act now".
