# DOGER — Government Efficiency & Budget Analysis

## Overview

Analyze government budgets, identify inefficiencies, propose AI-driven modernization solutions, and inspire community contributions toward more efficient public services. Named after the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) model, this skill helps citizens, analysts, and public servants apply technology-forward thinking to government operations.

## When to Use

- Analyzing a government budget to identify waste, duplication, or inefficiency
- Proposing AI and automation solutions for government processes
- Evaluating the cost-effectiveness of existing government programs
- Crowdsourcing ideas from the community for efficiency improvements
- Comparing government spending across departments, agencies, or jurisdictions
- Building a business case for technology modernization in government
- Identifying opportunities for shared services and consolidation
- Tracking government spending against outcomes and performance metrics
- Inspiring citizen engagement in government efficiency initiatives

## How It Works

Share a budget document, describe a government process, or outline a public sector challenge. The skill analyzes the information for efficiency opportunities, proposes technology-driven solutions, and structures findings for community contribution and stakeholder buy-in. The approach emphasizes practical, implementable improvements over abstract critiques.

## Steps

### 1. Budget Analysis Framework

- **Obtain budget data:** agency/department budgets, line-item detail, historical spending
- **Categorize spending:** personnel, contracts/consultants, IT systems, facilities, programs, overhead
- **Identify inefficiency patterns:**
  - Duplicate systems across departments (e.g., multiple HR platforms, separate IT contracts)
  - Legacy technology with high maintenance costs
  - Manual processes that could be automated
  - Under-utilized facilities or assets
  - Vendor lock-in and non-competitive procurement
  - Programs with unclear outcomes or impact metrics

### 2. AI & Technology Solutions Assessment

- **Process automation candidates:**
  - Form processing and data entry
  - Scheduling and appointment management
  - Customer service and inquiry response (chatbots, knowledge bases)
  - Document review and compliance checking
  - Payment processing and reconciliation
  - Reporting and analytics generation
- **Modernization opportunities:**
  - Cloud migration vs. on-premise data centers
  - Shared services platforms across agencies
  - Open-source alternatives to proprietary software
  - API-first architecture for inter-agency data sharing
  - Digital self-service portals replacing in-person or paper processes
- **Cost-benefit analysis:** implementation cost, annual savings, break-even timeline, risk assessment

### 3. Efficiency Recommendation Structure

For each finding, provide:
1. **Issue:** What's inefficient and why
2. **Evidence:** Data, spending figures, comparisons
3. **Proposed solution:** Specific technology or process change
4. **Implementation path:** Timeline, stakeholders, required resources
5. **Projected impact:** Cost savings, time savings, service improvement metrics
6. **Risk mitigation:** Potential obstacles and how to address them

### 4. Community Contribution Model

- **Open-source efficiency:** Share analysis frameworks and tools publicly
- **Citizen reporting:** Channels for residents to flag inefficiencies they encounter
- **Hackathons and challenges:** Structured events where technologists tackle specific government problems
- **Transparency dashboards:** Public-facing spending and outcome trackers
- **Cross-jurisdiction sharing:** Best practices and proven solutions shared across cities, states, and agencies

### 5. Stakeholder Communication

- **For agency leadership:** Executive summary with top 3-5 opportunities and ROI
- **For elected officials:** Constituent impact framing — how savings translate to better services
- **For the public:** Clear, jargon-free explanations of changes and benefits
- **For employees:** How automation improves their work, not just replaces it (re-investment in higher-value tasks)

### 6. Sample Efficiency Analysis Prompts

- "Analyze this department budget for IT spending duplication"
- "Identify processes in this agency that could be automated"
- "Compare per-capita spending across similar jurisdictions"
- "Propose a shared services model for these 3 departments"
- "Estimate the cost of manual paper processing vs. digital automation for this process"
- "What open-source alternatives exist for this proprietary government system?"

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Focusing only on cuts, not improvement** — Efficiency is about better outcomes per dollar, not just spending less. Frame proposals around service improvement.
2. **Ignoring implementation complexity** — Government procurement and change management take time. Propose phased approaches.
3. **Neglecting the human impact** — Automation proposals should include re-skilling and transition plans for affected employees.
4. **Over-promising AI capabilities** — Be realistic about what AI can and cannot do. Over-promising undermines credibility.
5. **Analyzing without stakeholder input** — Frontline employees often know exactly what's broken. Include their perspective in any analysis.
6. **Treating all spending as waste** — Some "inefficiency" is actually necessary redundancy, compliance, or equity investment. Distinguish waste from intentional policy choices.
