# Insurance Coverage Playbook

Domain knowledge for fighting coverage disputes as a policyholder. This is operational understanding, not legal advice — every dispute theory here gets validated against real opinions (via `case-law-research`) and licensed counsel before it goes into a draft.

## 1. How auto insurers actually operate

- **Underwriting** decides whether to take the risk and at what price. It runs on *applications*: garaging address, drivers in the household, prior losses, credit/insurance score. Anything the application gets wrong is a future coverage weapon for the insurer — but the insurer must *act* on it through proper channels, not just sit on it.
- **Policy services / notices** run on systems, not judgment. Cancellation notices, nonpayment bills, and underwriting memos are often generated by different systems on different timelines — which is exactly how a policyholder ends up with a July 24 cancellation date and an August 3 cure deadline in two different letters. Contradictory system-generated deadlines are real and exploitable.
- **Claims** is a separate silo from underwriting. The adjuster who denies your claim usually had nothing to do with the cancellation — but the denial still has to be *correct*, and "the policy was cancelled" is only a good denial if the cancellation itself was valid.
- **Money flows matter:** earned premium, installment fees, late fees, cancellation fees, and final bills. A "final bill" that says payment "will not reinstate" the policy is a company position, not a legal conclusion — read it as evidence of the company's posture, not as law.

## 2. The Iowa framework (as encoded; verify against current statute)

### Iowa Code chapter 515D — cancellation of auto policies
- Insurers face **restricted reasons** for mid-term cancellation of an auto policy *after* it has been in force 60 days.
- **Under-60-day exception:** a policy in force fewer than 60 days is a *new* policy the insurer may still be underwriting — cancellation is materially easier for the carrier. (This is why the "invalid reason" theory died on Patrick's policy: it was brand new, effective May 13, 2026.)
- **Notice requirements** (mailing, content, effective dates) are formal — a defective notice is a live issue even when the reason is valid. Always pull the mailing certificate/log.
- Cancellation for **nonpayment** vs **underwriting reasons** follow different tracks with different cure rights. Know which track you're on.

### Iowa Insurance Division (IID) complaint process
- Filed online; the Division **sends the complaint and all attachments to the insurer** and requests the insurer's response. Timeline is typically **30–45 days**.
- The Division can investigate and seek resolution, but a complaint **does not guarantee reinstatement or payment** — it is regulatory pressure and a paper trail, not a court order.
- Because the insurer sees everything: every sentence and every attachment must be reviewed for truth, completeness, and counsel-only material *before* submission. Never attach a PRIVATE-marked file to a complaint without explicit authorization.

## 3. Coverage-dispute anatomy (the recurring patterns)

1. **Garaging / misrepresentation vs. correct-and-rerate.** Insurers price on where the car sleeps. When the garaging facts change or were wrong, the carrier's remedy ladder is (weakest to strongest): re-rate the premium → nonrenew → cancel mid-term for material misrepresentation → rescind. Argue the carrier skipped rungs: it should have *corrected and re-rated* rather than canceling, especially when the policyholder offered to fix the information.
2. **Estoppel / detrimental reliance.** If the insurer's own communications led the policyholder to reasonably stop trying to cure (e.g., a "your policy recently canceled" email arriving *before* a stated cure deadline, or a final bill saying payment "will not reinstate"), the carrier may be estopped from insisting on strict compliance. Elements to document: the misleading communication, the date it arrived relative to the deadline, and what the policyholder did (or stopped doing) in reliance.
3. **Notice defects.** Was the notice mailed? When? To which address? Does the policy's notice clause match what happened? Get the mailing certificate and the carrier's activity log — don't take the notice's word for its own mailing.
4. **Contradictory deadlines.** Two letters, two dates, one policyholder. Map every deadline from every notice onto one timeline; contradictions deprive a policyholder of a fair opportunity to cure and read badly for the carrier in front of a regulator.
5. **What the carrier knew and when.** If the carrier's own systems flagged an address issue at *issuance* yet issued the policy anyway (e.g., an underwriting form noting address history), that undercuts a later "we just discovered this" cancellation narrative.

## 4. Key Iowa doctrines (summaries — VERIFY against downloaded opinions)

- **Reasonable expectations.** Iowa courts have held that ambiguous policy language is construed to honor the objectively reasonable expectations of the insured. Associated names in the case file: *Clark-Peterson* and *Boelman v. Grinnell Mutual* — **treat these as leads, not citations**, until `case-law-research` downloads and verifies the actual opinions. Never put an unverified case name in a draft.
- **Contra proferentem (ambiguity against the drafter).** Standard insurance-law principle: ambiguous terms are read against the insurer that wrote them. Useful wherever a notice or policy term is genuinely ambiguous (e.g., "verify the address where you keep your vehicles" — which address?).
- **Waiver / estoppel (general).** A carrier that acts inconsistently with its stated position (accepting premium after a purported cancellation, sending mixed messages about reinstatement) may waive the position.

## Guardrails

- Statutes and doctrines summarized here are **starting points**. Confirm current law before any filing or deadline math.
- Never present a playbook summary as a holding. The playbook explains *how insurers think*; only downloaded opinions establish *what courts held*.
