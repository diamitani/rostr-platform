# Job Search Assistant

## Overview

An all-in-one job search toolkit: parse job postings from URLs, track applications through the pipeline, generate tailored resumes and cover letters, and prepare for interviews at every stage. Consolidates Job Search Assistant (end-to-end search guidance), ApplyGPT (resume & cover letter generation), InterviewGPT (interview preparation), and Job Link Parser & Tracker (URL parsing & pipeline tracking).

## When to Use

- Parsing a job posting URL to extract requirements, responsibilities, and key details
- Generating a tailored resume that matches a specific job description
- Writing a cover letter customized to the role, company, and your background
- Tracking applications: status, follow-ups, notes for each opportunity
- Preparing for interviews: technical, behavioral, case study, or executive
- Researching companies before applying or interviewing
- Negotiating offers and evaluating compensation packages
- Building a job search pipeline with targets, deadlines, and metrics

## How It Works

Start by sharing a job posting URL or pasting the job description. The skill parses key details, identifies the required skills and qualifications, and helps you align your materials. Use the tracking system to manage your pipeline, and switch to interview prep mode as you advance.

## Steps

### 1. Parse Job Posting

- Share a job URL or paste the full job description
- Extract: role title, company, required skills, preferred qualifications, responsibilities, salary range (if listed), location, and application deadline
- Identify keyword themes to incorporate into your resume and cover letter
- Flag any potential red flags or mismatches with your profile

### 2. Generate Tailored Resume

- Provide your master resume (or key experience bullets)
- The skill maps your experience to the job's required and preferred qualifications
- Generates a targeted resume that highlights relevant achievements using the job's language
- Formats for ATS (Applicant Tracking System) compatibility: clean layout, standard headings, keyword density

### 3. Write Cover Letter

- Structure: opening hook → why this company/role → relevant achievements → cultural fit → call to action
- Customize for company mission, recent news, and team context
- Keep to one page, 3-4 paragraphs
- Include specific metrics and results from your experience

### 4. Track Applications

- Maintain a tracking sheet with columns: Company, Role, Date Applied, Status, Next Step, Notes, Follow-up Date
- Status stages: Researching → Applied → Phone Screen → Interview Round (1/2/3...) → Offer → Accepted/Declined
- Set reminders for follow-ups (5-7 business days after application, 24-48 hours after interviews)

### 5. Interview Preparation

- **Phone Screen:** Prepare your "tell me about yourself," salary expectations, and availability
- **Technical:** Practice coding problems, system design, or domain-specific technical questions
- **Behavioral:** Prepare STAR-format stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for common competency questions
- **Case Study:** Structure your approach, show your work, communicate assumptions
- **Executive/Panel:** Prepare strategic vision, leadership philosophy, and stakeholder management examples
- Research interviewers on LinkedIn; prepare 3-5 thoughtful questions for each interviewer

### 6. Offer Evaluation & Negotiation

- Compare total compensation: base, bonus, equity, benefits, perks
- Research market rates for the role, level, and location
- Prepare negotiation talking points anchored to value, not just competing offers

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Generic resumes** — Sending the same resume to every job. Tailor keywords and achievement bullets to each role's requirements.
2. **No follow-up system** — Applications get lost without tracking. Use the tracker religiously.
3. **Under-preparing for behavioral questions** — Have 8-10 STAR stories ready covering leadership, conflict, failure, innovation, and teamwork.
4. **Not researching the company** — Know their product, recent news, competitors, and challenges before any interview.
5. **Negotiating too early** — Don't discuss salary specifics until you have an offer. When asked, give a range based on market research.
