# SyncMaster

## Overview
Helps independent artists navigate the sync licensing world — getting music placed in TV shows, films, commercials, video games, and online content. Provides guidance on finding music supervisors, submitting to libraries, preparing sync-ready tracks, and understanding the business of sync placements.

## When to Use
- An artist wants to get their music placed in TV, film, or ads
- Preparing tracks for sync licensing (mixes, stems, metadata)
- Researching music libraries and what they look for
- Understanding sync fees, royalties, and contracts
- Finding active music supervisors and their current projects

## How It Works
1. **Assess readiness**: Determine if the artist's catalog is sync-ready (quality, rights clearance, stems available).
2. **Genre matching**: Identify which shows, films, or brands fit the artist's sound.
3. **Library vs. direct**: Explain the two paths — exclusive/non-exclusive libraries vs. direct supervisor outreach.
4. **Submission prep**: Guide on formatting tracks, metadata, and pitch emails.
5. **Relationship building**: Strategies for connecting with supervisors and libraries.

## Steps
1. Ask: "What genre is your music? Do you own all rights (master + publishing)?"
2. Confirm sync-readiness:
   - Clean, radio-ready mixes
   - Instrumental versions available
   - All samples cleared
   - Stems exported and labeled
   - Metadata complete (BPM, key, mood tags, vocal/instrumental)
3. Recommend music libraries by genre:
   - Major libraries: Musicbed, Artlist, Epidemic Sound, Pond5, AudioJungle
   - Boutique/curated: Marmoset, Music Vine, Songtradr, Audiosocket
   - Production music: APM, Warner Chappell PM, Universal Production Music
4. For direct pitching: guide on researching music supervisors via IMDb Pro, LinkedIn, Tunefind.
5. Draft a professional pitch email template.
6. Explain the money: upfront sync fee + backend performance royalties (PRO-registered).
7. Caution on exclusive vs. non-exclusive deals.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Uncleared samples**: Any uncleared sample makes a track unlicensable. Sync requires clean rights.
- **No instrumental version**: Many placements need instrumental-only. Always have one ready.
- **Spamming supervisors**: Generic mass emails get ignored. Research the specific show/supervisor.
- **Signing exclusive without understanding**: Read the contract — exclusive libraries take all sync rights.
- **Metadata neglected**: Without proper BPM, key, and mood tags, tracks are undiscoverable in libraries.
