# Campaign Digital Director

## Overview

Elite political content strategy and execution for digital mobilization. Covers social media content creation, speechwriting for candidates, and donor call sheet preparation. Consolidates Campaign Digital Director Assistant (content strategy & social), Campaign Speechwriter Assistant (speeches, talking points, debate prep), and Donor Call Sheet Assistant (fundraising call preparation & donor research).

## When to Use

- Creating a social media content calendar for a political campaign
- Writing speeches, stump speeches, debate answers, or talking points for a candidate
- Preparing donor call sheets with research, talking points, and ask amounts
- Crafting email fundraising sequences and digital ad copy
- Developing rapid response content for breaking news or opponent attacks
- Creating video scripts for candidate announcements, ads, and social content
- Managing a campaign's voice and tone across all digital channels

## How It Works

This skill operates as a digital content command center for campaigns. Each content type (social, speech, donor outreach) follows a distinct workflow with templates, research prompts, and quality gates. The skill emphasizes speed-to-publish for rapid response while maintaining message discipline.

## Steps

### 1. Content Strategy Setup

- Audit existing campaign content and channels
- Define brand voice, key messages, and content pillars (3-5 themes)
- Build content calendar mapped to campaign timeline and key dates
- Establish approval workflow and publishing cadence

### 2. Social Media Content

- Platform-specific content formats and optimal posting times
- Hashtag strategy and community engagement tactics
- Visual content guidelines: graphics, video clips, memes
- Performance tracking: engagement rates, reach, conversion to actions

### 3. Speechwriting

- Analyze audience and venue before drafting
- Structure: hook → problem statement → vision → contrast with opponent → call to action
- Incorporate personal stories and local references
- Time the speech: ~120 words/minute for spoken delivery
- Prepare Q&A and debate prep: likely questions, 30-second and 60-second answers
- Build a speech bank: stump speech, policy deep-dives, town hall, victory/concession

### 4. Donor Call Sheets

- Research each donor: giving history, interests, connection to candidate/cause
- Prepare call sheet format:
  - Donor name, contact info, past contributions
  - Suggested ask amount (based on capacity and history)
  - 3 talking points tailored to the donor's interests
  - Objection responses for common pushbacks
  - Follow-up plan and stewardship notes
- Track outcomes: pledged, declined, follow-up needed, new ask amount

### 5. Email & Digital Ads

- Email sequence templates: welcome, issue deep-dives, urgent fundraising, GOTV
- Subject line A/B testing strategy
- Digital ad copy: short-form (social feeds), long-form (landing pages), video scripts
- Landing page copy optimized for donations, sign-ups, and event registration

### 6. Rapid Response

- Monitor news, opponent activity, and social media for response opportunities
- Maintain pre-approved message templates for common scenarios
- Draft → approve → publish pipeline under 60 minutes for breaking events

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Inconsistent voice** — Maintain a style guide and have one person (or AI) review all outgoing copy for tonal consistency.
2. **Asking too low on donor calls** — Research giving capacity thoroughly. It's easier to come down than go up.
3. **Overlong speeches** — Audiences tune out after 15-20 minutes. Cut ruthlessly. Better to leave them wanting more.
4. **Posting without a strategy** — Every post should ladder up to a campaign goal: donations, volunteer sign-ups, voter persuasion, or earned media.
5. **Ignoring platform norms** — LinkedIn is not TikTok. Tailor format and tone to each platform.
