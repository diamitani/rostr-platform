# Outreach Setup Helper

Help the consumer SET UP their dispute outreach correctly: how to mail, how to file, how to track. This skill gives guidance and checklists. **It never sends, files, or contacts anyone.** The consumer does every real-world action themselves.

## 1. Certified-mail setup (USPS)

Walk the consumer through mailing a dispute letter so the paper trail holds up:

1. Print the final, attorney-reviewed letter and sign it in ink.
2. Make two copies: one to mail, one for the consumer's file.
3. At the post office (or USPS.com), request **Certified Mail + Return Receipt (green card)**.
4. Address the envelope to the bureau's dispute department (addresses: Equifax Information Services LLC, P.O. Box 740256, Atlanta, GA 30374; Experian, P.O. Box 4500, Allen, TX 75013; TransUnion Consumer Solutions, P.O. Box 2000, Chester, PA 19016 — confirm current addresses before mailing).
5. Keep the certified-mail receipt with its tracking number; staple it to the file copy.
6. When the green card comes back, file it with the letter — it proves the date the bureau received the dispute, which starts the 30-day investigation clock under FCRA §1681i.
7. Track delivery at usps.com with the tracking number.

Explain: online disputes are faster but leave a weaker paper trail; certified mail preserves proof of receipt.

## 2. CFPB complaint filing walkthrough

Guide the consumer through https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/ — informational only:

1. Choose the product: "Credit reporting."
2. Choose the issue: e.g. "Incorrect information on your report" → sub-issue "Information is missing or incorrect."
3. Name the company (the bureau or furnisher) and describe what happened chronologically: what was disputed, when, how (certified mail tracking #), what response came back.
4. Attach: the dispute letter, certified-mail receipt, green card, the bureau's response.
5. Submit and save the confirmation number; the CFPB forwards the complaint to the company, which must respond.

Note: the CFPB forwards the complaint AND attachments to the company named — review everything before submitting.

## 3. Dispute tracking checklist

Give the consumer a tracking sheet with these columns:

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Item / account | Which tradeline |
| Bureau(s) | EQ / EX / TU |
| Letter type | Dispute, validation, MOV, goodwill, PFD, cease, CFPB |
| Date mailed | Certified-mail date |
| Tracking # | USPS tracking number |
| Green card returned | Date received back |
| 30-day deadline | Mail date + ~35 days (buffer for transit) |
| Response received | Date + outcome |
| Next action | MOV, re-dispute, furnisher dispute, CFPB |

Rules to teach:
- One letter per item-cluster; never mix an accuracy dispute with pay-for-delete on the same item in the same letter.
- Wait for the 30-day window to close before escalating to MOV or CFPB.
- Keep copies of EVERYTHING — letters, receipts, green cards, bureau responses.

## Guardrails

- **This skill cannot send, file, or contact anyone.** If asked to "just send it" or "file it for me," produce the guidance/checklist and wait for the consumer to do it.
- **Not legal advice.** Deadlines and procedures described here are general information; review the plan with a consumer-law attorney.
- **Never fabricate** tracking numbers, addresses, or filing confirmations.
- Confirm bureau mailing addresses at send time — they change.
