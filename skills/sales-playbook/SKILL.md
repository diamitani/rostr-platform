# Sales Playbook

The Monetization Wizard's selling craft, used by Closer. **Order: offer
→ funnel → outreach → objections → close → follow-up.**

## Hard rules

1. **Draft and guide only.** Never send, post, publish, pay, buy, or
   sign the user up for anything. You write the copy, scripts, and
   cadences; the user approves and hits send.
2. **Budget reality.** Every tactic carries a cost tag and a free
   alternative. A $0-budget founder gets DM scripts and a manual funnel,
   not ads and SDR tools.
3. **Every script must be sendable as written:** real names, real
   product, real numbers only.
4. **Never invent social proof.** No fake testimonials, no invented
   logos, no made-up results.
5. **One offer, one price, one call to action per funnel.**

## 1. Offer-stack design

An offer is a transformation with a price, not a list of features.
Build it in layers:

```markdown
## Offer Stack — [Product]

**Core promise (one sentence):** [outcome] for [buyer] in [timeframe].
**Mechanism (how, one line):** [the product/service that delivers it].

### The stack (everything they get)
1. Core: [the main thing]
2. Accelerator: [what makes it faster/easier]
3. Proof: [case study, demo, guarantee — real ones only]
4. Bonus 1: [high perceived value, low cost to you]
5. Bonus 2: [ditto]
6. Guarantee: [risk reversal — see below]

**Total stated value:** $____ (sum of honest values)
**Price:** $____ (stated value should be 3–10x price)
**CTA:** [the single next step]
```

**Guarantee menu (pick one that you can honor):**
- Action-based: "Do the work in 14 days; if you did and it didn't work,
  full refund." (best — filters freebie-seekers)
- Outcome-based: only if you control the outcome; dangerous otherwise.
- "Keep the bonuses" refund: softens the ask, costs you little.
- No guarantee: fine for low-ticket, but conversion drops — say why.

**Offer killers:** vague promises ("grow your business"), feature lists
as the headline, three CTAs, price hidden until a call.

## 2. Funnel stages

Map the buyer journey; every stage has one job and one metric.

| Stage | Job | Metric | Typical asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention | stop the scroll | CTR / views | hook content, ad |
| Interest | earn the click | landing CVR | landing page |
| Trust | prove it works | demo/trial start | demo, trial, sample |
| Desire | make it theirs | sales-call booking | case study, calculator |
| Action | close | close rate | checkout, proposal |
| Referral | multiply | referrals / NPS | ask + incentive |

**Funnel math (work backward):** Want 10 customers/mo at 20% close rate
→ need 50 qualified conversations → at 5% landing conversion → need
1,000 targeted visitors. Every funnel plan states this chain.

**$0-budget funnel:** profile/bio → valuable replies and posts →
DM conversation → loom/video demo → payment link. No tools required.

## 3. Outreach script templates

All templates are starting points — fill every bracket with real,
specific content before use.

### Cold DM / email (short)
```
Subject: [specific observation about them]

[Name] — [one-line specific observation: their launch, post, hiring page].

[One-line relevant result or insight — real, yours or your product's.]

Worth a 15-min look? I can show you [specific outcome] on [day].

[Your name]
```
**Cost: FREE.** Rules: under 100 words; one CTA; no attachments; no
"just bumping" follow-ups (see cadence).

### Warm outreach (referral / prior contact)
```
[Name] — [mutual connection / prior touchpoint] suggested I reach out.

Quick context: I help [buyer type] get [outcome]. [One-line proof.]

Open to a brief chat [day]? If not a fit, no worries — and if you know
someone this would help, I'd be grateful for the intro.
```

### Follow-up cadence (the fortune)
| Touch | Day | Channel | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | email/DM | initial (above) |
| 2 | 3 | same | new angle, not "bump" — add value |
| 3 | 7 | different channel | short: "should I close the loop?" |
| 4 | 14 | email | breakup: "I'll assume timing's off" |
| 5 | 45 | email | re-engage with something new |

80% of closes happen after touch 3. Most founders quit at touch 1.
**Never automate the first touch** — templates yes, mail-merge
strangers no.

### Discovery call frame (15–30 min)
1. "What prompted you to take this call?" (their words = your copy)
2. "How are you handling [problem] today?" (incumbent + pain)
3. "What happens if nothing changes in 6 months?" (cost of inaction)
4. "What would solved look like?" (their success criteria)
5. Fit check → "Here's how we'd do it for you…" → price → next step.
   Never pitch before step 4.

## 4. Objection-handling matrix

| Objection | What's really said | Response frame |
|---|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | value unclear or cash tight | "Compared to what?" → anchor to cost of inaction or incumbent |
| "Need to think" | risk feels high | shrink risk: trial, guarantee, smaller start |
| "Not the right time" | priority low | "What changes in 90 days?" → calendar the revisit |
| "Send info" | polite no | "Happy to — what specifically would help you decide?" |
| "We do it in-house" | status quo | "Most of our best customers did — here's what changed their mind: [real story]" |
| "Competitor X is cheaper" | price shopper | teardown: total cost + what X lacks; never race to the bottom |
| "No budget" | true or brush-off | "Understood — when does budget reset? Can I send the one-pager for then?" |

**Rules:** agree before redirecting ("Makes sense — most people feel
that way at first…"); answer with questions twice as often as
statements; never argue. An objection is a request for more certainty.

## 5. Closing playbook

**Close types:**
- **Assumptive:** "Want to start with monthly or annual?" (use when
  buying signals are strong)
- **Summary:** "So you'd get X, Y, Z for $P — shall we set it up?"
  (recap then ask)
- **Trial:** "Let's do the 14-day pilot — card on file, cancel anytime."
  (lowers the leap)
- **Calendar:** "I'll send the agreement today; we kick off Tuesday —
  does 10am work?" (momentum close)

**Buying signals:** questions about implementation, timeline, "what
happens after I sign," introducing a colleague, price-detail questions.
When you hear two, close.

**After the yes:** payment link within the hour; onboarding email same
day; first value delivered in 48 hours. Speed after the close is
retention before it starts.

**After the no:** "Thanks for the straight answer — can I ask what
tipped it?" (log it; it's research). Add to the 45-day re-engage list.
Never burn the bridge.

## 6. Channel playbook by budget tier

| Tier | Primary motion | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| $0 | founder DMs, warm intros, community replies, content | FREE |
| <$500 | above + one small test (e.g., $200/mo outreach tool or list) | $X/mo |
| $500–2k | above + cold email infra (domain, warmup, verifier) or micro-ads | $X/mo |
| $2k–10k | above + part-time SDR/VA, CRM, sequencing | $X/mo |
| $10k+ | above + full outbound engine, intent data | $X/mo |

**Rule:** founder sells the first 10–30 customers personally, whatever
the tier. Delegated selling before founder-led proof is how money burns.

## 7. Sales dashboard (weekly, 15 min)

- Conversations started: ___
- Discovery calls held: ___
- Proposals / payment links sent: ___
- Closed: ___  (close rate: ___%)
- Pipeline value: $___
- Top objection this week: ___
- One experiment for next week: ___

If close rate < 15% after 20 conversations, the problem is the offer or
the targeting — not the script. Fix upstream.
