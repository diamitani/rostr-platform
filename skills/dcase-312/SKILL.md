# DCASE 312 — Chicago Cultural Affairs

## Overview

Access resources, events, and opportunities from the Chicago Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events (DCASE). Find cultural events across the city, discover grant programs for artists and arts organizations, locate city-wide cultural programs, and identify community engagement opportunities. DCASE is the city department that produces some of Chicago's largest festivals and manages the Chicago Cultural Center, Millennium Park, and public art programs.

## When to Use

- Finding free or low-cost cultural events in Chicago (festivals, concerts, exhibitions, film screenings)
- Searching for artist grants, residencies, and professional development opportunities
- Locating DCASE-managed venues: Chicago Cultural Center, Millennium Park, Clarke House Museum
- Identifying public art installations and cultural programming by neighborhood
- Learning about city-sponsored festivals: Chicago Blues Festival, Jazz Festival, World Music Festival, etc.
- Finding opportunities for artists to perform, exhibit, or vend at city events
- Accessing Chicago Film Office resources for filming permits and location support
- Discovering neighborhood cultural plans and community arts partnerships
- Connecting with cultural advisory councils and public input opportunities

## How It Works

This skill serves as a navigation guide to DCASE resources. It helps you find what you're looking for — events happening this week, grant deadlines approaching, or programs relevant to your neighborhood. For real-time information, web search is used to access current listings on chicago.gov/dcase and related sites.

## Steps

### 1. Find Cultural Events

- Search current and upcoming events at:
  - Chicago Cultural Center (78 E. Washington St.) — free daily programs
  - Millennium Park — summer music, film series, art installations
  - Neighborhood festivals and city-sponsored events
- Filter by: date, neighborhood, event type (music, dance, theater, visual art, film, family)
- Check holiday and seasonal programming: Christkindlmarkt, Taste of Chicago, summer dance

### 2. Explore Grant Programs

- **Individual Artists Program (IAP):** Grants for Chicago-based artists to develop their practice
- **CityArts Program:** Operating and project grants for arts organizations
- **Neighborhood Access Program:** Funding for cultural programming in underserved communities
- **Chicago Presents:** Sponsorship for large-scale cultural events
- Check application windows, eligibility requirements, and funding priorities
- Review past grant recipients for examples of successful applications

### 3. Access Venues & Public Art

- Chicago Cultural Center: free exhibitions, concerts, tours, learning programs
- Millennium Park: Lurie Garden, Pritzker Pavilion, Crown Fountain, Cloud Gate ("The Bean"), public programs
- Clarke House Museum: Chicago's oldest house, tours and history programs
- Public Art Collection: 500+ works across 40+ neighborhoods
- Year of Public Art initiatives and temporary installations

### 4. Engage with Cultural Programs

- **Nights Out in the Parks:** Free cultural events in neighborhood parks (summer season)
- **Chicago Film Office:** film permits, location database, production resources
- **Music Festivals:** Blues Fest, Jazz Fest, World Music Festival, Gospel Fest, House Music Festival
- **Maxwell Street Market:** Sunday market with vendors, food, and live music
- **Neighborhood Cultural Plans:** Community-driven cultural development in partnership with DCASE

### 5. Artist & Creative Professional Resources

- Artist registry and performer database sign-up
- Calls for artists: public art commissions, exhibition opportunities, festival bookings
- Professional development workshops and networking events
- Cultural space finder: venues and rehearsal space directory
- Creative industry job board and contractor opportunities

### 6. Community Input & Advisory Councils

- Cultural Advisory Council meetings (public, open attendance)
- Public input sessions for cultural planning
- Neighborhood cultural planning processes
- Volunteer opportunities at cultural events and venues

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Assuming DCASE only does downtown events** — DCASE programming is city-wide through Nights Out in the Parks and neighborhood cultural plans.
2. **Missing grant deadlines** — Most DCASE grants have annual cycles. Check early and set reminders for application windows.
3. **Not checking venue hours** — Chicago Cultural Center and Millennium Park spaces have specific hours and occasional closures for private events.
4. **Overlooking free programming** — The majority of DCASE events are free, especially at the Cultural Center and in parks.
5. **Waiting until the last minute for film permits** — Film Office permits require lead time, especially for street closures or large productions.
