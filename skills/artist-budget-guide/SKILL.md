# Artist Budget Guide

## Overview
Helps independent artists build realistic, line-item budgets for their music projects. Breaks down costs across recording, production, marketing, distribution, touring, and release expenses — with realistic ranges for each.

## When to Use
- Planning a recording project (single, EP, album) and need a cost estimate
- Budgeting for a tour or live show run
- Preparing a grant application or crowdfunding campaign
- Deciding how to allocate a fixed budget across competing needs

## How It Works
1. **Scope the project**: Determine the type of release, number of tracks, and timeline.
2. **Category breakdown**: Walk through each cost category with realistic ranges.
3. **Prioritize**: Help the artist allocate spending where it matters most for their goals.
4. **Deliver a budget spreadsheet**: Line items with low/mid/high estimates.

## Steps
1. Ask: "What are you planning — single, EP, album, tour, or all of the above?"
2. Ask about total available budget or if they need cost discovery.
3. Break down costs by category:
   - **Recording**: Studio time ($30-$150/hr), producer, session musicians, engineer.
   - **Mixing & mastering**: $200-$1,500/track for mixing, $50-$200/track for mastering.
   - **Artwork & visuals**: Cover art ($200-$2,000), press photos ($300-$1,500), music video ($500-$10,000+).
   - **Distribution**: DistroKid/TuneCore/CD Baby ($20-$50/year), physical pressing ($1,000-$5,000).
   - **Marketing & PR**: Playlist pitching, social ads, PR firm ($500-$5,000+).
   - **Touring**: Transportation, lodging, per diems, merch production, venue costs.
4. Create a prioritized budget: must-haves vs. nice-to-haves.
5. Deliver a clean budget document with totals and notes.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Underestimating hidden costs**: Mastering, ISRC codes, PRO registration, mechanical licenses.
- **No contingency**: Always add 15-20% buffer for unexpected expenses.
- **Overspending on recording, underspending on marketing**: An amazing song nobody hears is a loss.
